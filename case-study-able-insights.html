From Scattered to Structured

Designing a scalable information architecture for an AI-powered research platform that transforms how analysts organize, refine, and share market intelligence reports.

The Story

Research teams drowning in data

Research teams were drowning in their own data. I designed an information architecture that turned chaos into clarity—transforming how analysts create, organize, and share market intelligence reports.

What started as scattered queries across multiple tools became a unified system where AI and human judgment work together. The result: a scalable report structure that handles everything from 50 to 5,000 companies without breaking.

The Challenge

Users were manually copying data between queries, losing context, and spending hours assembling reports from fragmented sources. There was no consistent way to build, refine, or share research.

The Solution

I designed an expandable report architecture with progressive disclosure—a "living container" that captures the entire research journey from initial query (937 companies) through filtering and refinement (339 → 42 companies) to final insights.

Understanding the Problem

The Research Workflow Was Broken

When I joined the project, analysts described their workflow like this: "I ask the AI a question, get 900 companies back, then spend the next 3 hours copying data into spreadsheets, cross-referencing sources, and trying to remember which filters I applied."

The core issues were clear:

No transparency

Users couldn't see how they got from 937 companies to their final 42. The filtering logic was invisible.

Loss of context

After applying 6 filters, analysts forgot which criteria removed which companies—making it impossible to explain their methodology to stakeholders.

Manual assembly required

Every report meant copying charts, tables, and summaries from different parts of the tool into PowerPoint or Word.

No repeatability

When a client asked to update last month's research, analysts had to start over from scratch—no saved workflows, no memory of the process.

What Was At Stake

This wasn't just about workflow efficiency—it was about trust and credibility. When analysts can't explain their methodology, stakeholders lose confidence in the insights. Research becomes defenseless opinion rather than evidence-based analysis.

The Innovation

A Report Architecture That Shows Its Work

I redesigned research as a progressive disclosure system where every step of the journey is visible, organized, and reusable.

Core Innovation: The Expandable Report Container

Instead of showing 937 companies in one overwhelming table, I created a three-tier structure:

  • Added by queries (937 companies) — Shows what the AI found initially
  • Removed by filtering (895 companies) — Makes the elimination process transparent
  • Final list (42 companies) — The refined, actionable result

Each tier is collapsible, with controls to "See all," "Hide," or drill into details. This creates a visual funnel that tells the story: "We started here, refined it this way, and ended up here."

Added by queries: 937 companies from initial AI results

Removed by filtering: 895 companies filtered out with applied criteria

Final list: 42 refined, actionable companies

Feature: Query Transparency

When users expand "Added by queries," they see exactly which questions they asked and which questions the AI added to help. This makes the collaboration between human and machine explicit:

  • "13 questions you asked to create the list"
  • "3 questions added with our help"

Every query shows how many companies it contributed (+42, +70, +61), creating a mental model of how the research built up.

Feature: Filter Visualization

The "Removed by filtering" section doesn't just hide companies—it shows why they were filtered out. Users see:

  • 6 filters were applied to this list
  • Revenue > $3M USD
  • Year Founded: 2000+
  • Employees: 20-1000

This transparency is critical when presenting to stakeholders: "We eliminated 895 companies because they didn't meet these 6 criteria."

Feature: Auto-Generated Domain Reports

Once users reach their final list, they can generate a "Domain Report"—a publication-ready document that includes:

  • Executive summary with key insights
  • Companies list with funnel visualization
  • Investment landscape charts
  • Technology cluster analysis
  • Market trends and highlights

The entire report is generated from the structured data, maintaining consistency and saving hours of manual work.

Guiding Principles

Information Architecture Foundations

Progressive Disclosure Over Immediate Complexity

Don't show 937 companies at once. Reveal information in layers as users need it. The default view shows summaries; drill-downs reveal details.

Make The Invisible Visible

Research methodology should be as transparent as the results. Show which queries ran, which filters applied, and how many companies each decision affected.

Design For Repeatability

Every report should be updateable. If a user runs the same research next month, they should be able to regenerate it with fresh data without remembering every step.

Respect The Human-AI Collaboration

The AI suggests queries, but humans decide which to use. The interface should celebrate this partnership, not hide it.

Design Process

From Discovery to Launch

Discovery: Understanding The Chaos

I analyzed existing user behavior data—looking at the actual questions users were asking, the filters they applied, and where they got stuck. I worked closely with our sales team to gather client feedback and understand pain points reported in support tickets.

The breakthrough: Research isn't a single query—it's a process that needs to be documented as it happens. The funnel from 937 → 42 companies needed to be visible at every step.

"By the time I finish a report, I can't remember how I got here. If my manager asks 'why did you exclude this company?' I have to guess." — From client feedback via sales team

Ideation: From Linear To Layered

My first sketches treated reports like documents: header, body, footer, done. But that model doesn't match how research actually works.

The pivot came when I started thinking about research as a funnel—or more accurately, a transparent tunnel that users travel through. Users needed to see the entire tunnel, not just their current position. Every stage should be collapsible but visible—so analysts could zoom out to see the big picture (937 → 42) or drill in to understand exactly what happened at each step.

This wasn't just about showing data—it was about building a narrative. The funnel tells a story: "We cast a wide net with AI, refined it with business criteria, and arrived at these 42 high-confidence targets."

Iteration: Testing With Real Data

I built interactive prototypes with real company data (339 agritech companies in one test, 141 AI companies in another). This exposed problems immediately and led to key solutions:

  • Problem: When all 937 companies were visible, users felt overwhelmed and gave up.
    Solution: Default to showing summaries with counts (937, 339, 42) and let users expand to see details.
  • Problem: Users didn't understand why companies disappeared when they applied filters.
    Solution: Created the "Removed by filtering" section that explicitly shows what got filtered out and why.
  • Problem: The query list was just text—boring and hard to scan.
    Solution: Added icons for query types (🔍 search, 🤖 AI-suggested) and contribution counts (+42, +70).

Collaboration: Building With Engineering

Working with engineers, we discovered that the expandable architecture had a bonus benefit: performance. By rendering only visible sections, we could handle massive datasets (5,000+ companies) without the browser choking.

This validated the design principle: progressive disclosure isn't just good UX—it's good engineering.

Design System

Visual Language & Components

Color System: Stage-Based Identity

The visual design needed to support, not compete with, complex data. I established a color-coding system where each funnel stage has its own identity:

  • Light Blue (#E8F4F8) — "Added by queries" (neutral, informative)
  • Peach (#FFE8D9) — "Removed by filtering" (warm, de-emphasized)
  • Mint Green (#D4F4DD) — "Final list" (fresh, actionable)

Typography & Data Display

Typography is Inter throughout—Medium weight for headings, Regular for body text, with tabular figures ensuring perfect numerical alignment in data tables.

Component Architecture

Every element was designed as a reusable component: stat cards, filter chips, company rows, and chart modules. This systematic approach paid immediate dividends—these components are now used across 12 different features in the platform.

The interaction model emphasizes progressive disclosure: smooth expand/collapse animations (200ms), clear hover states, and "See all/Hide" toggles that give users control over information density.

Key Learnings

What This Project Taught Me

Research isn't linear—it's fractal

Users need to zoom in (see all 937 companies) and zoom out (just show me the 42 finalists) fluidly. The expandable structure enables this mental model.

Users don't want AI to be invisible

They want to understand what the AI did. Showing "3 questions added with our help" builds trust. Hiding AI contributions creates suspicion.

Systems thinking scales products

When you design one component well and make it reusable, you unlock 10 other features. The "Explore Process" pattern is now used for query history, saved searches, and report versioning.

Transparency is a feature, not a bug

Users initially wanted faster filtering. What they really wanted was to understand and defend their filtering decisions. Making the process visible solved the real problem.

Reflection

The Path Forward

This case study showcases a design challenge I'm passionate about: taking invisible, complex processes and making them visible, understandable, and scalable.

The expandable report architecture isn't just a UI pattern—it's a new mental model for how AI-assisted research should work. By designing for transparency, repeatability, and progressive disclosure, we created a system that respects both the power of AI and the judgment of human experts.

What's Next: Scheduled Reports

The architecture we built enables something users have been requesting since day one: scheduled reports. Because every report captures its complete methodology (queries + filters + parameters), users can now set reports to auto-regenerate on a schedule—weekly, monthly, or quarterly.

When a scheduled report runs, it will:

  • Re-execute the exact same queries with fresh data
  • Apply the same filters to new company datasets
  • Highlight what changed since the last run (new companies added, companies that no longer qualify)
  • Send email notifications with a "what's different" summary

This transforms research from a one-time activity into an ongoing monitoring system. The expandable architecture makes this possible because it preserves the complete research methodology, not just the final results.

The work continues—there's always more to improve—but I'm proud of building a foundation that scales with the product's ambitions.
